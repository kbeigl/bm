= Scotland Yard Map

Clearly each scenario needs a map at this early stage.
The scenario map is a bounding area for the scenario 
and is used to restrict the movement of the players.
Even better, the scenario should provide a digital map of the area, 
which can be used for navigation and visualization.
It is worth while to understand 
the map of London and how it is represented in the game. 
The game board is a graph with stations as nodes and streets as edges.

Each Player of the game can move along the streets of the map of London on a digital map. +
Each move is a track between two stations on the map, which are the edges of the graph of the map.


== mapping the world

A GPS Tracker is a fascinating little matchbox to scan the world. +
We attach it to some thing, measure its motion to explore the globe or our neighborhood. +
Birds and Wales show us their travel around the globe for best seasonal conditions.

The GPS Traces of racing cars are more than points on the racing track. +
They imply physics like the grip of the tires, the airpressure on the vehicle and much more. +
Just imagine some computer creating these tracks from all vehicle parameters. +
How likely do the measured tracks match the simulated ones?

A GPS Tracker Software or Simulator opens even more perspectives for a tracking tool. +
In theory you can transform any shape defined in cartesion systems to geographic coordinates. +
It might make sense to define a house in geo coordinates as it doesn't change its location, +
but measuring it with a GPS unit is practically impossible.

It _is_ possible with much more effort, like differential GPS for centimeter precision and much more.
Many of these efforts are made in the sciences of geodesy and cartography.
Navigation Systems have become Driver Systems, which push the demand for high density maps.
These include _road furniture_ and other tiny details used for driving information.

By using *Digital Maps* exclusivly defined with only [_lat,lon_] points 
we get a reliable source for the actual positions of geostationary objects.
On an Open Source Platforms like OpenStreetMap all contributers 
have to agree on the final coordinates of a road or building.

[IMPORTANT]
====
Digital Maps provide a *Common Ground* like a Game Board for Real World Scenarios.
====

== graph vs map

In the previus section we have created the Scotland Yard Game Board as a Graph. +
For convenience we have added geo coordinates to the stations,
but this is not necessary for game control. +
The moves of the players are defined by the edges of the graph and not by the streets. +
In the figure below you can see the Graph projected onto the open street map of London. +
Thanks to precise georeferencing the stations are in the correct geographic position + 
and some *Links* are roughly following the larger streets, + 
while other links are straight lines going through buildings and parks.

We want more. +
We want the players to move along the streets of the map of London on a digital map +
and provide the geometry for our trackers to follow.

Navigation systems usually work with a graph and encoded links. +
Each link can have large numbers of attributes, like its length, 
to use in complex algorithms. 

A navigation system does not require the real road geometry,
simply because a person is steering the car by sight. +
The nodes of the graph are also called maneuver points, 
where a driver has to make a decision. 

A self driving car does need the geometry of the road to steer the car along the road. +
That is what we want to provide with our digital map. +

== outlook

When two cars - in the real world - are approaching each other head on 
at high speeds on a narrow road, drivers don't need a map to avoid a collesion. 
They need to look ahead and react to the situation.

For a Driver Assistance System a map can help to tell that the two cars
are speeding on the Autobahn, but each one on his lane in his direction. 
Large physical dividers between the lanes. 
No warning required from ADAS.

In automotive industry this is often referred to 
as the _Digital Horizon_ or _eHorizon_. +
So the system can warn the driver of a car about upcoming 'events' and obstacles on the road. +
This would not work without digital map data.

Therefor a realistic scenario does not need a common map for the actors. +
We will develop Real World Objects, which can be navigated independently. +
Like in real life every one can use his own map and navigation system, +
or simply look through the windshield and follow the road.

The moving objects define the world.
The map is for visualization and navigation.
A RealObject can also install navigation software for osm.
The synchronization is achieved by a common coordinate system, i.e. map and a common time base. 


== public transport

We have used the public transport network of London to create a realistic game map for Scotland Yard. 
This is start.
On a longer term we could access public transportation data servers 
to get the actual positions of the vehicles and provide live subways for London or any other place in the world.

Google provides a good source for public transport data in the
https://developers.google.com/transit/gtfs[General Transit Feed Specification (GTFS)].

Another source is the https://transitfeeds.com/website, +
which provides a large collection of GTFS feeds from around the world.
